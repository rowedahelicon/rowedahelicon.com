Timeline

December 12th
I came out as trans.
Though I had been open about considering myself non-binary for a long time, I never figured that also meant I was technically trans. I was told it was, and suddenly the idea became comfortable to me, so I decided to make it official.

December 27th
I booked my first HRT appointment.
Having come out as trans, the idea of taking the steps to go on HRT suddenly felt stronger. It's something that was quietly sitting in my head for what feels like forever, but never one I was strong enough to go for. I was sent a post about burning designs into rocks, and the energy of "you can just do things" hit me. I booked an appointment with my local Planned Parenthood for January 3rd.

January 3rd
I went to my first ever HRT appointment.

So, the day comes, I go, and holy shit. I was so afraid, but it all felt so...natural. The people there were so nice, and they were so kind and welcoming. They spoke so plainly and matter of factly. They didn't question why I was there to do what I was doing, just what I wanted out of it, do I have a support group, etc.

They addressed my concerns, gave me a timeline of expectations, wrote me a prescription and setup a follow up appointment, and sent me on my way.

I then left my local target which had my pharmacy. I decided to use it because my family doesn't, I don't want to tell them any of this yet. I go to the target, and just walk around for 20 minutes while I wait. Walking around, I finally felt...different. I felt like a different person, but a real one, I felt small and weak, soft, exactly how I've always felt, but at the surface level. I felt brave, because I was taking control of my life, and not just dishing out some stupid punishment for a change. The only bug I was fixing was my own, wrong body, ez fix.

It took a day to really hit for me, I was mostly in shock over how quick this all was ultimately. One night I'm sitting here thinking "wow...can I really do this?" and starting tomorrow, I'm starting it all. Years of confusion and anguish and one quick appointment was all I needed. The best part, my insurance (which I didn't even know I had) covered all of it. When I was at the clinic, the form had a section for my preferred name and pronouns, I wrote down the name I've always had stuck in my head, and they called me by that name so naturally. I've never heard anyone call me by it before, it was immediately recognizable and comfortable, but still wild that I finally got to hear it.
